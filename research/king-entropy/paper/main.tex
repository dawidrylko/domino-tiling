\begin{abstract}
We compute the entropy per site of the perfect matchings (dimer coverings) of the king's
graph, the square lattice with both diagonals; equivalently, of the domicule tilings of
rectangular boards counted in OEIS A239264 and A239273. Row transfer matrices on cylinders of
circumference up to $25$, split by a conserved parity, give
\[
  s = 0.5655119110413253 \pm 6\cdot 10^{-16},
\]
with an error bar supported by several extrapolation models, backtests and an
extended-precision check of the rounding. We prove $0.545343749727 \le s \le 0.586216605404$
with computer-assisted Collatz--Wielandt certificates;
the upper bound uses a Fibonacci bound for the matchings across a cut. We also give the surface
free energy of a free edge, $\sigma = 0.242017935766251 \pm 9\cdot 10^{-15}$, and the
exponential decay of the splitting between the two parity sectors, with decay length
$\xi \approx 1.23$. Finally we conjecture that the logarithm of the growth rate of the free strip
of width $2q$ is convex in $q$ for $2q \ge 22$; this would raise the rigorous lower bound to
$s \ge 0.565511911035$, and it holds numerically up to $2q = 24$. The stronger log-convexity of
$q \mapsto Z(2q, m)$ holds in all $1451$ cases we checked, but it is not a structural property:
it fails on the triangular lattice, under arbitrarily small asymmetric changes of the diagonal
weights, and for equal diagonal weights $x = 3/2, 2, 3$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A \emph{domicule} is a pair of cells of the square grid that share a side or a corner. A domicule
tiling of a $K \times L$ board is a partition of its cells into domicules; two diagonal domicules
may cross inside a unit square, so the $2\times 2$ board has $3$ tilings. Equivalently, it is a
perfect matching of the \emph{king's graph} on the board, the graph whose edges join cells at
chessboard king distance $1$. The numbers $Z(K, L)$ of such tilings form the array
A239264 of the OEIS \cite{oeisA239264}, and $Z(2n, 2n)$ is A239273 \cite{oeisA239273}; related
entries such as A239265, A239268 and A243510 \cite{oeisA239265,oeisA239268,oeisA243510} record
further exact counts. None of these entries gives the growth constant, i.e.\ the entropy per
site
\[
  s = \lim_{K, L \to \infty} \frac{\log Z(K, L)}{KL}.
\]

For dominoes (the square lattice) $s$ is known exactly, $G/\pi$ with Catalan's constant $G$, by
the Pfaffian method of Kasteleyn and of Temperley and Fisher \cite{kasteleyn1961,
temperleyfisher1961}. Kasteleyn's method needs a Pfaffian orientation, which exists for planar
graphs, and the triangular lattice (one diagonal per square) is planar and was solved in the
same way \cite{fms2002}. The king's graph is not planar: the $5 \times 5$ board already has
$72 > 3\cdot 25 - 6$ edges. No Pfaffian or determinantal solution of the king's dimer model is
known. Sandvik and Moessner \cite{sandvikmoessner2006}, and before them Sandvik
\cite{sandvik2003}, studied exactly this model (dimers on the nearest- and
next-nearest-neighbour bonds of the square lattice) by Monte Carlo simulation; they note that it
is not solvable by Kasteleyn's method and do not give its entropy. Wildeboer et
al.\ \cite{wildeboer2026} solve a different, constrained dimer model on lattices with crossed
plaquettes. Finch's errata \cite[\S 5.12]{finch2020errata} list a constant
$1.34254\ldots 1.34265$ for the square lattice with ``crossed diagonal bonds''; it belongs to
hard squares (independent sets of the king's graph), not to dimers. Series expansions for
dimer and monomer--dimer problems go back to Nagle \cite{nagle1966} and Gaunt \cite{gaunt1969};
we have only seen their abstracts, which do not mention the king lattice. To our knowledge no
numerical value of $s$ has been published.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Results.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $s = 0.5655119110413253 \pm 6\cdot10^{-16}$ (\S\ref{sec:value}), i.e.\ $2s = 1.131023822082651$
  per dimer and $e^{s} = 1.760348694041978$. The error bar is not rigorous.
\item Rigorously, $0.545343749727 \le s \le 0.586216605404$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bounds}).
\item The surface free energy of a free edge is $\sigma = 0.242017935766251 \pm 9\cdot10^{-15}$
  (\S\ref{sec:surface}).
\item On even cylinders the two parity sectors split by $\Delta_w \approx e^{-w/\xi}$,
  $\xi \approx 1.23$; all finite-size corrections are exponentially small (\S\ref{sec:z2}),
  in contrast with the critical square lattice.
\item The conjecture (L$_\infty$) that $q \mapsto \log\lambda_{2q}$ is convex for $2q \ge 22$
  would give the rigorous bound $s \ge 0.565511911035$ (eleven correct digits). The stronger
  log-convexity (L) of $q \mapsto Z(2q, m)$ holds in every case we tested at unit weights, but
  we show that it is not structural: it fails for diagonal weights $(1, \tfrac{9}{10})$ and for
  equal diagonal weights $x = \tfrac32, 2, 3$, and a Krein-signature argument for the left--right
  symmetry explains why (\S\ref{sec:L}).
\end{enumerate}
The same code reproduces the exactly known entropies of the square and triangular lattices
(\S\ref{sec:checks}). All scripts, outputs and the map from every number in this paper to the
file it comes from are in the repository (\S\ref{sec:repro}).

\section{Model, transfer matrix and parity sectors}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Definitions}
The king's graph has vertex set $\Z^2$, and $(r, c) \sim (r', c')$ if
$\max(|r - r'|, |c - c'|) = 1$. The board $B(K, L) = \{0, \dots, K-1\} \times \{0, \dots, L-1\}$
has $K$ rows and $L$ columns; $Z(K, L)$ is the number of perfect matchings of the induced
subgraph ($Z = 0$ if $KL$ is odd), and $Z(K, L) = Z(L, K)$. The cylinder of circumference
$w \ge 3$ identifies column $c$ with $c + w$; its count is $Z_{\mathrm{cyl}}(K, w)$. Let
\[
  \bar s = \limsup \frac{\log Z(K, L)}{KL}, \qquad \underline s = \liminf \frac{\log Z(K, L)}{KL},
\]
over $K, L \to \infty$ with $KL$ even. That $\underline s = \bar s$ is standard for dimer models
on $\Z^2$-periodic graphs (a sub/superadditivity argument); Theorem~\ref{thm:bounds} bounds
$\underline s$ and $\bar s$ separately and does not use it.

For a strip of width $k$, stacking boards gives $Z(2i + 2j, k) \ge Z(2i, k) Z(2j, k)$, so by
Fekete's lemma \cite{fekete1923} the limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda}
  \lambda_k^2 = \lim_{j \to \infty} Z(2j, k)^{1/j} = \sup_j Z(2j, k)^{1/j}
\end{equation}
exists. We call $\lambda_k$ the growth rate of the free strip and write $a_k = \log \lambda_k$.
Placing strips side by side gives $\lambda_{k_1 + k_2} \ge \lambda_{k_1}\lambda_{k_2}$, so
$a_k/k \to s^* = \sup_k a_k/k$.

\subsection{Transfer matrix}
We scan a strip of width $w$ cell by cell in row-major order. A cell is either already covered,
or it is matched to one of its later neighbours: right $(r, c+1)$, down-left $(r+1, c-1)$, down
$(r+1, c)$ or down-right $(r+1, c+1)$, if that neighbour exists and is free. The state records
the occupancy of the next $w + 2$ cells; on the cylinder the neighbours are taken modulo $w$ and
one extra bit records the cell $(r+1, w-1)$ claimed by $(r, 0)$ through the wrap-around
down-left edge. A row is $w$ cell steps, $T = T_{w-1}\cdots T_0$, and
\[
  Z(K, w) = e_0^{\top} T^K e_0,
\]
with $e_0$ the empty state. Between rows only the $w$ bits of the next row can be set, so $T$
acts on $2^w$ states: the set $A \subseteq \{0, \dots, w-1\}$ of cells of the next row already
covered from the current one. The implementation uses reshaped array views instead of index
arrays and is bit-identical to a direct one.

\subsection{Parity sectors}
Let $A_r$ be the set of cells of row $r$ covered from row $r - 1$ and $h_r$ the number of
dimers inside row $r$. Counting the cells of row $r$ gives $w = |A_r| + 2h_r + |A_{r+1}|$, so
\[
  |A_{r+1}| \equiv w - |A_r| \pmod 2.
\]
Hence $T^2$ preserves the parity $p$ of $|A|$; for even $w$ so does $T$, for odd $w$ the map $T$
exchanges the two sectors and $T^2$ has the same nonzero spectrum on both. We write
$\lambda^{(p)}_w$ for the square root of the leading eigenvalue of $T^2$ on sector $p$ of the
cylinder, and $f_w^{(p)} = \log \lambda^{(p)}_w / w$. On the cylinder $|A|$ is the number of
dimers crossing a closed loop around the axis, and for even $w$ its parity is conserved: this is
the topological $\Z_2$ sector familiar from dimer models on non-bipartite lattices. For even $w$
the two sector values differ, and they bracket $s$ in all our data: $f^{(0)}_w > s > f^{(1)}_w$.

For free strips only the sector of $e_0$ enters $Z$, and the spectral radius of $T$ is not the
growth rate $\lambda_w$ in general: for $w = 6, 10, 14, 18$ the other sector has the larger
eigenvalue (e.g.\ $w = 6$: $\log\lambda = 2.909568954808860$ there, against
$a_6 = 2.909403518708946$).

\subsection{Computation}
We use float64 power iteration on $T^2$ restricted to one sector, from a random positive start
vector (fixed seed), normalised every step, stopped when the estimate of $\log \lambda$ changes
by less than $4\cdot10^{-15}$ for three consecutive iterations (19 iterations for $w \ge 20$).
Afterwards the Collatz--Wielandt bracket $\min_i (T^2 v)_i / v_i \le \lambda^2 \le \max_i (T^2
v)_i / v_i$ over the support of $v$ is evaluated; for $w = 20, \dots, 25$ it closes to the last
digit of $\log \lambda$. Widths $w \le 19$ take minutes; $w = 25$ (one sector suffices for odd
$w$) works on vectors of $2^{28}$ doubles and needs $8.4$\,GiB and $22.5$ minutes on four cores.

\emph{Rounding.} We recomputed the odd cylinders $w = 15, 17, \dots, 25$ in x87 extended
precision (64-bit mantissa). The float64 values differ by at most $4.1\cdot10^{-17}$ per site, and

reproduces both sector values to $1.8\cdot10^{-15}$.

\[
  \boxed{\,s = 0.5655119110413253 \pm 6\cdot10^{-16}\,}
\]
This is an estimate, not a bound. For comparison, the exact counts of the square boards up to
$20\times20$ alone give about six digits (a fit $\log Z(L, L) = sL^2 + aL + b$ on $L = 16, 18, 20$
gives $0.565512081672$).

\section{Rigorous bounds}\label{sec:bounds}

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:bounds}
$0.545343749727 \le \underline s \le \bar s \le 0.586216605404$.
\end{theorem}

For comparison, the Kahn--Lov\'asz bound (the number of perfect matchings of a graph is at most
$\prod_v (d_v!)^{1/(2 d_v)}$; unpublished by its authors, proved in \cite{alonfriedland2008}),
with all degrees $d_v \le 8$, gives
$\bar s \le \log(8!)/16 = 0.662787681422$.

Both bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounds} come from strips of width $w = 24$ and a numerical
certificate whose validity does not depend on how it was found. Table~\ref{tab:bounds} shows how
they improve with $w$. Since $\log \lambda_w / w = s + f(w)/w$ with $f(w) \to -2\sigma$
(\S\ref{sec:surface}), the lower bound approaches $s$ like $2\sigma/w = 0.484/w$; the upper bound
does like $0.497/w$.

\subsection{Lower bound}

\begin{lemma}[superadditivity]\label{lem:super}
For even $K$ and $L = kw + r$ with $0 \le r < w$: $Z(K, L) \ge Z(K, w)^k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Perfect matchings of $k$ strips $B(K, w)$ placed side by side, completed by vertical dominoes in
the last $r$ columns ($K$ is even), are distinct perfect matchings of $B(K, L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Collatz--Wielandt, lower]\label{lem:cwlow}
Let $A = T^2$ be the free-strip transfer of width $w$, so that $Z(2j, w) = (A^j e_0)_0$. Let
$v \ge 0$ with $v_0 > 0$, $\supp v \subseteq \supp A^n e_0$ for some $n$, and $Av \ge c\,v$
componentwise. Then $Z(2(n + j), w) \ge c^j v_0 / C'$ for all $j$, where
$C' = \max_{i \in \supp v} v_i / (A^n e_0)_i < \infty$. In particular $\lambda_w^2 \ge c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$v \le C' A^n e_0$ componentwise and $A \ge 0$, so
$Z(2(n+j), w) = (A^j A^n e_0)_0 \ge (A^j v)_0 / C' \ge c^j v_0 / C'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the lower bound]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:super}, for even $K$,
\[
  \frac{\log Z(K, L)}{KL} \ge \frac{\lfloor L/w\rfloor}{L}\,\frac{\log Z(K, w)}{K}
  \longrightarrow \frac{\log\lambda_w}{w} \qquad (K, L \to \infty);
\]
for odd $K$ (then $L$ is even) exchange the roles of the sides. So $\underline s \ge a_w / w$ for every $w$, and
$\underline s \ge s^*$. The certificate of \S\ref{sec:float} gives
$a_{24}/24 \ge \frac{1}{48}\log c_{24} \ge 0.545343749727$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Upper bound}
Cut $B(K, kw)$ into $k$ strips $S^{(1)}, \dots, S^{(k)}$ of width $w \ge 2$ (columns
$jw, \dots, jw + w - 1$). King edges change the column by at most one, so every edge of a perfect
matching $X$ lies inside a strip or joins column $jw - 1$ to column $jw$ (an edge across cut $j$).
Let $A_j$ be the rows of the cells of column $jw - 1$ matched across cut $j$ (right side of
$S^{(j)}$), $B_{j+1}$ the rows of the cells of column $jw$ matched across cut $j$ (left side of
$S^{(j+1)}$), and $B_1 = A_k = \emptyset$. Let $N(B, A)$ be the number of perfect matchings of
\[
  B(K, w) \setminus \bigl(\{(b, 0) : b \in B\} \cup \{(a, w-1) : a \in A\}\bigr),
\]
and $M(A, B)$ the number of perfect matchings of the bipartite graph on $A \sqcup B$ with
$a \sim b$ iff $|a - b| \le 1$.

\begin{lemma}[cut identity]\label{lem:cut}
$Z(K, kw) = \sum \prod_{j=1}^{k} N(B_j, A_j) \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} M(A_j, B_{j+1})$, the sum over all
$A_1, B_2, A_2, \dots, B_k$ with $B_1 = A_k = \emptyset$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $w \ge 2$ the sets are well defined. The restriction of $X$ to strip $j$ is a perfect matching
of $S^{(j)} \setminus (B_j \cup A_j)$, and its restriction to cut $j$ a perfect matching between
$A_j$ and $B_{j+1}$; conversely every such tuple of pieces is a perfect matching of $B(K, kw)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[crossing matchings]\label{lem:fib}
If $|A| = |B| = n$ then $M(A, B) \le F_{n+1} \le \varphi^n$, where $F_1 = F_2 = 1$ are the
Fibonacci numbers and $\varphi = (1 + \sqrt 5)/2$; $M(A, B) = 0$ if $|A| \ne |B|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $a_1 < \dots < a_n$, $b_1 < \dots < b_n$ and $M(n) = \max M(A, B)$. \emph{$a_1$ is matched to
$b_1$ or $b_2$}: if $a_1 \sim b_j$ with $j \ge 3$, then $b_1 \le b_3 - 2 \le a_1 - 1$, while every
$a \ne a_1$ has $a \ge a_1 + 1 \ge b_1 + 2$, so $b_1$ has no partner. \emph{If $a_1 \sim b_2$},
then $b_1 \le b_2 - 1 \le a_1$ needs a partner $a_i \ge a_1 + 1$ with $a_i \le b_1 + 1 \le a_1 + 1$,
so $a_i = a_1 + 1$, $b_1 = a_1$, and the pair $a_2 b_1$ is forced. Thus every matching contains
$a_1 b_1$ (the rest is a matching of $n - 1$ against $n - 1$ cells) or both $a_1 b_2$ and
$a_2 b_1$ (the rest is $n-2$ against $n-2$), so $M(n) \le M(n-1) + M(n-2)$ with
$M(0) = M(1) = 1$, i.e.\ $M(n) \le F_{n+1}$. Finally $F_{n+1} \le \varphi^n$ by induction.
\end{proof}

The bound is attained by $A = B$ an interval. Put $z = \sqrt\varphi$ and
\[
  W(K, w) = \sum_{A, B} N(B, A)\, z^{|A| + |B|},
\]
the weighted number of matchings of a strip in which a cell of the first or last column may be
``covered from outside'' at weight $z$.

\begin{lemma}[strip inequality]\label{lem:strip}
$Z(K, kw) \le W(K, w)^k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} replace $M(A_j, B_{j+1})$ by $\varphi^n = z^{|A_j|} z^{|B_{j+1}|}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fib}). The factor $z^{|A_j|}$ belongs to strip $j$ and $z^{|B_{j+1}|}$ to strip
$j+1$, so each summand is at most $\prod_j N(B_j, A_j) z^{|B_j| + |A_j|}$. Dropping the constraints
$|A_j| = |B_{j+1}|$ and $B_1 = A_k = \emptyset$ adds only nonnegative terms, and the sum
factorises over the strips.
\end{proof}

The transfer of \S\ref{sec:model} computes $W$ when a cell in column $0$ or $w-1$ may also be
covered from outside at weight $z$: $W(K, w) = e_0^\top T_z^K e_0$, $T_z \ge 0$.

\begin{lemma}[Collatz--Wielandt, upper]\label{lem:cwup}
If $A = T_z^2$, $v \ge 0$, $v_0 > 0$ and $Av \le Cv$ componentwise (including $(Av)_i = 0$
where $v_i = 0$), then $W(2j, w) \le C^j$ for all $j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$e_0 \le v/v_0$ and $A \ge 0$, so $A^j e_0 \le A^j v / v_0 \le C^j v / v_0$; take the entry $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the upper bound]
Let $K, L \to \infty$ with $KL$ even. If $K$ is odd then $L$ is even and $Z(K, L) \le Z(K+1, L)$
(add a row of horizontal dominoes), so we may take $K = 2j$. Write $L = kw + r$, $0 \le r < w$.
Cutting off a last strip of width $r$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:strip} gives
$Z(K, L) \le W(K, w)^k W_r(K)$, where $W_r$ counts the configurations of a width-$r$ strip with
weighted outside covers; crudely $W_r(K) \le (5 + 2z)^{Kr}$ (per cell: already covered, one of at
most four later partners, or one of at most two outside covers). By Lemma~\ref{lem:cwup},
\[
  \frac{\log Z(K, L)}{KL} \le \frac{k}{L}\,\frac{\log C_w}{2} + \frac{w \log(5 + 2z)}{L}
  \longrightarrow \frac{\log C_w}{2w}.
\]
The certificate of \S\ref{sec:float} at $w = 24$ gives $\bar s \le 0.586216605404$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Floating-point certificates}\label{sec:float}
The certificates use float64 vectors $v$ produced by power iteration; how $v$ was obtained does not
matter. For the lower bound $v$ is the normalised iterate $A^n e_0$; floating point never creates a
positive entry from an exact zero (all terms are nonnegative), so $\supp v \subseteq \supp A^n e_0$.
The code computes $y = \fl(Av)$, asserts $v_0 > 0$, $v_i > 10^{-280}$ on $\supp v$ and (upper
bound) $y_i = 0$ off $\supp v$, and sets
\[
  c = \min_{i \in \supp v} \fl(y_i / v_i)\,(1 - 10^{-12}), \qquad
  C = \max_{i \in \supp v} \fl(y_i / v_i)\,(1 + 10^{-12}).
\]
Each cell step writes every entry as a sum of at most six nonnegative terms (already covered,
outside cover times $z$, right, down-left, down, down-right), so over the $2w$ steps of $A$,
$\fl(Av)_i \in (Av)_i\,[(1-u)^{12w}, (1+u)^{12w}]$ with $u = 2^{-53}$: a relative error of at most
$12wu\,(1 + o(1)) = 3.2\cdot10^{-14}$ at $w = 24$, plus one rounding in the division. All weights
are at least $1$ ($z$ is rounded up, so $T_{\fl(z)} \ge T_{\sqrt\varphi}$ entrywise) and the
entries on the support exceed $10^{-280}$, so there is no underflow and a float entry vanishes iff
the exact one does. Since $10^{-12} \gg 3.2\cdot10^{-14} + u$, the inequalities $Av \ge cv$ and
$Av \le Cv$ hold exactly. The logarithms are taken with 30-digit arithmetic and the results are
rounded outward to 12 decimals.

\emph{Audit.} The following were checked in exact arithmetic: the cut identity and both strip
inequalities on $58$ boards ($K \le 8$, $kw \le 12$); $\max M(A, B) = F_{n+1}$ for all $A, B$ in
columns of length $m \le 11$, and the branching step for $m \le 8$; the transfer with weight $z$
against brute force as a polynomial in $z$ ($162$ evaluations); and, for $w = 2, \dots, 12$, the
float ratios against exact rational ratios for the same float vector ($|\text{float}/\text{exact}
- 1| \le 1.7\cdot10^{-16}$). The test has power: with $z = 0$ the strip inequality fails on all
$40$ boards with $k \ge 2$. On these small boards $z = 0.5$ would suffice; the weight $\sqrt\varphi$ is
needed by the proof, not by the data.

\section{Checks on solvable lattices}\label{sec:checks}
The transfer code has a switch for the square lattice (no diagonals) and the triangular lattice

\section{A convexity conjecture for the strip growth rates}\label{sec:L}

\subsection{Statement and consequence}
\begin{conjecture}[L$_\infty$]\label{conj:Linf}
For the king's graph, $q \mapsto a_{2q} = \log\lambda_{2q}$ is convex on $2q \ge 22$, i.e.\
$a_{2q+2} - 2a_{2q} + a_{2q-2} \ge 0$ for $2q \ge 24$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cond}
(L$_\infty$) implies $\underline s \ge 0.565511911035$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under (L$_\infty$) the increments $b_q = a_{2q+2} - a_{2q}$ are nondecreasing for
$q \ge 11$; since $a_{2q}/(2q) \to s^*$, they increase to $2s^*$, so $s^* \ge b_{11}/2$, and
$\underline s \ge s^*$ by the proof of the lower bound. Finally $b_{11} \ge \log\lambda_{24}^{\mathrm{low}}
- \log\lambda_{22}^{\mathrm{up}}$, with the certified lower bound for $w = 24$ (\S\ref{sec:float},
minus $10^{-14}$ for the float storage of the per-site value) and a certified upper bound
$\log\lambda_{22} \le 11.95722617139012$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:cwup} with $z = 0$. This gives
$0.565511911035$.
\end{proof}

The gap between this bound and the estimate of $s$ is $5.7\cdot10^{-12}$: all $132$ candidates
$(a_{2q+p} - a_{2q})/p$ with $2q \le 22$, $2q + p \le 24$ lie below $s$, the largest being
$0.5655119110355926$ ($2q = 22$, $p = 2$). This is the Calkin--Wilf form of the lower bound
\cite{calkinwilf1998}, whose standard proof via reflection positivity does not apply here
(\S\ref{sec:obstructions}). The unconditional bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounds} remains
$\underline s \ge 0.545343749727$.

\emph{Evidence for (L$_\infty$).} The second differences of $a_{2q}$ are positive at every
$2q = 4, 6, \dots, 24$ and decay geometrically: $4.69\cdot10^{-5}$, $3.99\cdot10^{-6}$, $5.53\cdot10^{-7}$,
$5.63\cdot10^{-8}$, $6.96\cdot10^{-9}$, $7.62\cdot10^{-10}$, $9.0\cdot10^{-11}$, $1.01\cdot10^{-11}$
at $2q = 10, 12, \dots, 24$ (from uncertified power iteration, float error about $10^{-15}$); so
numerically (L$_\infty$) holds at its first instance $2q = 24$, and beyond that it is
extrapolation. A Prony fit of these differences finds two modes, a positive one $0.1147^q$ (the
transfer gap, $e^{-2\cdot1.083}$) and an alternating one $(-0.072)^q$; their ratio is $0.007$ at
$2q = 24$ and shrinks by a factor $0.63$ per step, stable over four fitting windows. The
convexity is robust to breaking the left--right symmetry: with diagonal weights $(1, x)$ for
$x = 0.95, 0.9, 0.75, 0.5$ and on the triangular lattice ($x = 0$) all second differences up to
$2q = 14$ are positive. It is not robust in the size of equal diagonal weights
(\S\ref{sec:notstructural}): for $x = \tfrac32, 2, 3$ on both diagonals it fails at $2q = 8$ and $2q = 12$.

\subsection{The stronger finite statement (L)}
For unit weights we also observed
\[
  \text{(L)}\qquad Z(2q, m)^2 \le Z(2q - 2, m)\, Z(2q + 2, m) \quad\text{for all } m, q \ge 1 .
\]
(L) implies (L$_\infty$): it makes $q \mapsto m^{-1}\log Z(2q, m)$ convex for each $m$; by
$Z(2q, m) = Z(m, 2q)$ and \eqref{eq:lambda} this tends to $a_{2q}$ along even $m$, and a
pointwise limit of convex sequences is convex. Table~\ref{tab:L} summarises the tests of (L):
exact integer counts by a transfer modulo several $60$-bit primes and the Chinese remainder
theorem, and for $m = 25, 26$ an x87 computation with a rigorous rounding bound (each cell step
writes a sum of at most five nonnegative terms, so the computed $Z$ is within a factor
$(1+u)^{5mk}$, $u = 2^{-64}$; the smallest certified margin is $1.237\cdot10^{-10}$). So (L) holds
on free strips of width $m \le 26$ and on cylinders of circumference $m \le 22$ in the stated
row ranges. The margin $\log Z(2q-2) + \log Z(2q+2) - 2\log Z(2q)$ decreases monotonically in $q$
for every $m$, by a ratio tending to $0.16$--$0.20$; there is no dip at intermediate $q$ and
nothing that shrinks with $m$.

We do \emph{not} conjecture (L). It is a numerical observation at unit weights, not a structural
property of the model: the next subsection shows that it fails under arbitrarily small
perturbations of the diagonal weights that break the left--right symmetry, and for equal
diagonal weights $x = \tfrac32, 2, 3$, which keep it.

\subsection{Why (L) is not structural}\label{sec:notstructural}
\emph{Triangular lattice.} On free triangular strips (L) fails from $m = 5$ on:
$Z(6, 5) = 6475$, $Z(8, 5) = 210560$, $Z(10, 5) = 6842465$ and
$210560^2 - 6475\cdot6842465 = 30552725 > 0$ (confirmed by brute force).

\emph{The left--right symmetry and its Krein signature.} Give the orthogonal edges weight $1$ and
the two diagonals weights $x_{\mathrm{dl}}, x_{\mathrm{dr}}$. The reflection $J\colon c \mapsto m - 1 - c$
of a strip of width $m$ exchanges the two diagonals, so it is a symmetry exactly when
$x_{\mathrm{dl}} = x_{\mathrm{dr}}$. Write the row transfer as $T = HM$, with $H$ the horizontal
dimers inside a row and $M$ the crossing kernel. Then $H = H^{\mathsf T} = JHJ$ and, for
\emph{any} diagonal weights, $M^{\mathsf T} = JMJ$; so $G = JM$ is symmetric and $GT$ is symmetric,
i.e.\ $T$ is self-adjoint for the indefinite form $G$. If $r_j$ is the right eigenvector of a simple
real eigenvalue, its weight in $Z(k, m) = (T^k)_{00}$ is
\[
  c_j = \frac{r_j(0)^2}{r_j^{\mathsf T} G\, r_j},
\]
so the sign of $c_j$ is the Krein signature of $r_j$. (Checked in exact arithmetic for $m \le 7$
and five weight sets, and against a direct eigendecomposition for $m \le 9$, relative error
$7\cdot10^{-13}$.) With equal weights the empty state is $J$-even, $Z$ sees only the $J$-even
sector, and there $G = M$; on the $J$-odd sector $G = -M$. Under the perturbation $(1, 1 - \varepsilon)$
the top $J$-odd level, which is $M$-positive, becomes visible with a \emph{negative} weight
$-K\varepsilon^2$ ($K = 0.70, 1.31, 1.63$ at $m = 4, 5, 8$, constant for
$\varepsilon = 10^{-3}, \dots, 3\cdot10^{-2}$). Wherever that level lies above the leading visible
$J$-even level ($m = 4$ and $m = 8$ among $m \le 11$), (L) must fail for large $q$, and it does
(exact): the weights $(1, \tfrac{9}{10})$ violate (L) at $m = 4$ from $2q = 56$, at $m = 8$ from
$2q = 86$ and at $m = 12$ from $2q = 112$, and $(1, \tfrac{19}{20})$ at $m = 8$ from $2q = 118$; the
onsets agree exactly with those predicted from the spectrum. Earlier tests of these weights
($m \le 16$, $2q \le 78$) had stopped just short of them. Clearly unequal weights fail much
earlier: $(1, \tfrac12)$ from $m = 5$ on ($14$ of $19$ values of $q$ at $m = 5$, $2q \le 38$),
$(1, \tfrac34)$ at $m = 9$ ($5$ of $19$, first at $2q = 30$). Restoring the symmetry restores (L)
in every tested case: the symmetrised transfer $H(M + M^{\mathsf T})/2$ has no violation, including
all cases above where $HM$ fails.

\emph{Equal weights are not enough.} With equal weights $x$ on both diagonals (L) holds for
$x \in \{0, \tfrac14, \tfrac12, 1, 2, 4, 10, 100\}$ on narrow strips ($m \le 9$, $2q \le 38$), but
not on wide ones. Exactly, $Z(8, m)^2 > Z(6, m)\,Z(10, m)$ for every $m$ with $35 \le m \le 60$
at $x = \tfrac32$, $21 \le m \le 60$ at $x = 2$ and $19 \le m \le 60$ at $x = 3$, while $x = 1$
and $x = \tfrac54$ give no violation at $2q = 8$ for $m \le 60$. These are the finite-width traces of a
failure of (L$_\infty$) itself: the second difference of $a_{2q}$ at $2q = 8$ is $-6.2\cdot10^{-4}$
at $x = \tfrac32$ and $-2.8\cdot10^{-3}$ at $x = 2$, and the differences alternate in sign. In the
language of the Prony fit above, the alternating mode, which at $x = 1$ is $0.007$ of the positive
one at $2q = 24$, is already comparable to it at $x = \tfrac54$ (ratios of consecutive second
differences $2.0$, $376$, $0.16$, $48$) and dominates at $x = \tfrac32, 2, 3$. So the symmetry $J$ is
necessary for (L) but not sufficient, and at $x = 1$ both (L) and (L$_\infty$) are quantitative
facts: $x = 1$ lies below a transition $x^* \le \tfrac32$. Any proof has to see this, which rules
out arguments that are uniform in the weights.

(L) is also specific to even row counts: for even $m$, $j \mapsto Z(2j + 1, m)$ is
log-\emph{concave} in $217$ of $220$ tested cases.

\subsection{Obstructions to standard proofs}\label{sec:obstructions}
\emph{Reflection positivity} fails for every reflection line of the king lattice. Between rows a
reflection maps one diagonal to the other, and the gluing form $M(A, B)$ (the crossing matchings of
\S\ref{sec:bounds}) is indefinite: its smallest eigenvalue is $1 - \sqrt2$ already for columns of
length $m = 3$, and the eigenvector $(1, -\sqrt2, 1)$ on $A = \{0\}, \{1\}, \{2\}$ is realised by
actual three-row half-boards (with weights $10, 4, 10$). This vector is $J$-even, so restricting
to the sector that $Z$ sees does not help: on the $J$-even sector $M$ has $1, 1, 3, 8, 20, 34, 93,
186$ negative eigenvalues for $m = 3, \dots, 10$. Through a line of vertices the kernel is
$[U \ne D]$ with eigenvalues $\pm1$. The transfer is not symmetric ($w = 3$:
$T[\emptyset \to \{1\}] = 2$, $T[\{1\} \to \emptyset] = 0$).
\emph{Moment sequences.} If $Z(2q, m)$ were a Stieltjes moment sequence, (L) would follow; but
$e_0$-visible eigenvalues of $T^2$ are non-real or negative from $m = 3$ on (small, at most
$0.047\,\lambda_1^2$ in modulus at $m = 10$), and the Hankel matrices have negative minors.
\emph{Total nonnegativity} of the transfer fails for the same reason, and the
Lindstr\"om--Gessel--Viennot lemma needs a planar path model that the crossing diagonals do not
provide. \emph{Interlacing} of the strip spectra would need a Hermitian structure, or a Pontryagin
space of small negative index, but $G$ has exponentially many negative directions. Monotonicity of
the gap gives the decay rate of the second differences, not their sign.

\subsection{Reformulations of (L)}
Superpose a matching $X$ of rows $[0, 2q)$ with a matching $Y$ of rows $[2, 2q+2)$ ($Z(2q)^2$
pairs), or $X'$ of $[0, 2q+2)$ with $Y'$ of $[2, 2q)$ ($Z(2q+2)Z(2q-2)$ pairs). The union $H$ has
degree one on the top rows $\{0, 1\}$ and the bottom rows $\{2q, 2q+1\}$ and degree two in between,
so it consists of paths and even cycles. In the first case top--top and bottom--bottom paths have
odd length and top--bottom (TB) paths even length; in the second all paths are odd. Each $H$ has
$2^{c(H)}$ labelings, $c(H)$ the number of cycles of length at least four, so
\[
  Z(2q-2)Z(2q+2) - Z(2q)^2 = \sum_{H:\,t(H) \ge 1} 2^{c(H)}
  \bigl([\text{all TB paths odd}] - [\text{all TB paths even}]\bigr),
\]
where $t(H)$ is the number of TB paths. We checked this identity pair by pair for small
$(m, q)$ (up to $3.4\cdot10^{6}$ pairs). The odd configurations win separately for every $t$
(e.g.\ $m = 5$, $q = 2$: $t = 2$: $1847660$ against $912656$; $t = 4$: $32616$ against $0$). So (L)
asks for a sign-reversing injection that flips the parity of all top--bottom paths; by
\S\ref{sec:notstructural} it would have to depend on the size of the diagonal weight. Cut-and-swap
involutions below the first row where the two boundaries agree (plainly or after the reflection
$J$) do pair off part of the configurations, but the unmatched remainder does not shrink with $q$.
Equivalently, cutting the board in the middle, (L) says that the indefinite form $M$ is positive
semidefinite on the span of two boundary vectors $u_{q-1}, u_{q+1}$ of half-boards.

\begin{problem}
Prove (L$_\infty$) for the king's graph; this would make the lower bound $s \ge 0.565511911035$
a theorem. A proof must distinguish unit diagonal weights from equal weights $x = \tfrac32, 2, 3$,
where (L$_\infty$) fails. The only route we see is a computer-assisted proof of the asymptotics
of the strip free energy $a_{2q}$, with the leading finite-size correction controlled to relative
accuracy about $0.6$ for every $2q \ge 22$.
\end{problem}
